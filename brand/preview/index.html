Yonder

A calm place to plan a trip with the people you're going with. A weekend in Lisbon or a month across Asia.

Plan the trip together.
Name

Five short names, one pick

Each works for any destination and any group size. Yonder won because it names the feeling of the whole app: the place you're all heading, not yet reached.

ChosenYonder

"Over there." Soft, six letters, no travel cliché, and nothing in it ties the product to one region.

Waymark

A trail marker. Clear and useful, but reads as hiking gear.

Hither

"Come here." A warm invitation, but easily misread as "hitter".

Plotline

Plotting a route, and the story of the trip. Sounds like a writing tool.

Legwork

Friends join legs of a trip. Clever, but "legwork" means chores.

Logo

A route that forks toward a point

The left branch is where someone joins; the right branch stops just short of the apricot dot, the place the group is heading. Keep that gap: it is the idea. Clear space around the logo is one dot diameter on every side.

Icons

Favicon and app icons

The favicon uses a heavier cut of the mark with its stem on a pixel edge, so 16 and 32 px stay sharp. App icons get a quiet dusk gradient; maskable icons keep all ink inside 34% of the width from center, well inside the 40% safe zone.

Color

Dusk, with one warm point

A cool, quiet evening palette. Indigo carries actions and focus; apricot is used once per screen at most, for the thing you're heading to next. Every text pair clears WCAG AA and every line and chart color clears 3:1 (run pnpm check).

Map

One line style per way of moving

Mode is carried by pattern and weight as well as hue, so routes still read for color-blind travelers and in grayscale. Each line sits on a casing in the page color so it holds up over busy tiles.
